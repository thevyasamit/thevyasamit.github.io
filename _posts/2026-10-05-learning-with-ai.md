AI has been trained on huge amounts of data and rewarded, again and again, for
getting things right. Ask it about one area of knowledge, like geography,
science, history or mathematics, and it is remarkably good. It explains things
clearly, in a way people can follow.

The trouble starts when you connect those areas.

## Where the pieces meet

Ask how geography shaped science, or how history influenced the way science
developed, and the question gets much harder. AI can still help here. It may
even show you connections you didn't know about or weren't looking for.

But this is also where ambiguity, interpretation and confusion creep in. A
single fact can be checked. A connection between fields is an argument, and
an argument can sound convincing and still be wrong.

## Divide and conquer

My answer to this is an old one from computer science: **divide and conquer**.

1. **Break** the topic into its individual parts.
2. **Understand** each part on its own.
3. **Bring** the parts back together and form your own view of the whole.

The reason this works is that it keeps you in charge of the reasoning. When a
subject is complicated, mixed or ambiguous, don't take whatever the AI
produces as the final answer. Use it to explore the pieces, question the
connections and challenge the assumptions. Then steer it, or correct it,
toward what actually makes sense.

AI can help you navigate complexity. You still have to own the navigation.

## Three questions

Whether I'm answering my own questions or explaining something to someone
else, three questions make almost anything clearer:

**What is it?**
Understand what the thing actually is. Define it, and know its basic parts.

**Why is it?**
Understand why it exists, why it matters, and what causes or principles sit
behind it.

**How do you use it?**
Understand how the knowledge can be applied, connected to other knowledge, or
used to make a decision.

Ask them about each piece first. Then ask them again about the whole.

## Thinking with AI, not depending on it

Start with the pieces. Understand them properly. Then connect them yourself.
And when the answer becomes unclear, don't hand your judgment over to the
machine. Use it as a tool to sharpen your own thinking.

That, I think, is the difference between working with AI and simply depending
on it. It's the same lesson as [artificial belief](/writing/artificial-belief/):
the understanding has to be yours.
